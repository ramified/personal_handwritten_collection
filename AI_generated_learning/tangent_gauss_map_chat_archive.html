Tangent Gauss Maps, Albanese Geometry, and Blow-ups — Discussion Archive

A structured archive of the discussion on tangent Gauss maps, Albanese geometry, Fano and Schoen surfaces, conormal incidence, excess terms, and blow-ups.

Notation fixed here. We write \(\mu^*I_Z\) for the pulled-back/extended ideal sheaf generated by the pulled-back functions. If the tensor-product pullback is needed, it is written explicitly as \(I_Z\otimes_{\mathcal O_Z}\mathcal O_{\widetilde Z}\).

Compact summary

  • \(\iota_Z:Z^r\to A^n\), \(V=T_0A\), \(\varphi:V^\vee\otimes\mathcal O_Z\to\Omega_Z^1\).
  • \(\gamma_Z:Z\dashrightarrow\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)\) and \(\lambda_Z:\Lambda^rV^\vee\to H^0(Z,K_Z)\).
  • For an immersion: \(K_Z^r=\deg(\gamma_Z)\deg_{\rm Pl}(\gamma_Z(Z))\).
  • For an immersion: \(\deg(\text{conormal Gauss})=\int_Zc_r(\Omega_Z^1)\).
  • For a generically immersive map, tangent and conormal formulas acquire different excess corrections.
  • The maximal-minor ideal satisfies \(\operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^r\varphi)=\mathcal I_BK_Z\).
  • Notation convention: \(\mu^*I_Z\) means the extended/pulled-back ideal generated by the pulled-back functions.

1. General setup and notation

User
I would like to prepare a talk about the tangent Gauss map. I want a general theory first, then specialize to Albanese cases. I use \[ \iota_Z:Z\to A,\qquad \dim Z=r,\quad \dim A=n,\qquad V=T_0A. \]
Assistant
The tangent Gauss map is \[ \gamma_Z:Z\dashrightarrow \operatorname{Gr}(r,V),\qquad z\mapsto d\iota_{Z,z}(T_zZ). \] The dual differential is \[ \varphi:V^\vee\otimes\mathcal O_Z\to\Omega_Z^1. \] Taking the top wedge gives \[ \lambda_Z:\Lambda^rV^\vee\to H^0(Z,K_Z). \] After Plücker, \(\operatorname{Pl}\circ\gamma_Z\) is the rational map defined by \(\operatorname{Im}\lambda_Z\). The basic invariants are \(\ker\lambda_Z\), the image \(Y_Z=\overline{\gamma_Z(Z)}\), and \(\deg(\gamma_Z)\).

2. Smoothness and generic immersivity

User
Should I suppose \(Z\) to be smooth?
Assistant
For the clean theory, assume \(Z\) smooth projective of dimension \(r\), and \(\operatorname{rk}(d\iota_Z)=r\) generically. Then \(\Omega_Z^1\) and \(K_Z\) are honest bundles. The Gauss map is rational in general and is a morphism if \(\iota_Z\) is immersive everywhere.

3. Albanese specialization

User
I want the general theory and then to specialize to the Albanese case.
Assistant
When \(A=\operatorname{Alb}(Z)\), one has \[ V^\vee\simeq H^0(Z,\Omega_Z^1), \] so \[ \lambda_Z:\Lambda^rH^0(Z,\Omega_Z^1)\to H^0(Z,K_Z) \] is intrinsic. For a general map \(Z\to A\), the image \[ W=\operatorname{Im}(V^\vee\to H^0(Z,\Omega_Z^1)) \] is the selected space of \(1\)-forms coming from the chosen abelian target. This is the right framework for Abel–Prym and the spin quotient examples.

4. Castelnuovo–de Franchis

User
How is the Castelnuovo–de Franchis theorem stated formally?
Assistant
If \(Z\) is smooth projective and there are linearly independent \[ \alpha,\beta\in H^0(Z,\Omega_Z^1) \] with \(\alpha\wedge\beta=0\), then there is a surjective morphism with connected fibres \[ f:Z\to C,\qquad g(C)\ge2, \] such that \(\alpha,\beta\in f^*H^0(C,K_C)\). For a surface, a nonzero decomposable element in the kernel of \[ \Lambda^2H^0(\Omega_Z^1)\to H^0(K_Z) \] therefore produces an irrational pencil.

5. The Fano surface

User
Why can we get \(\lambda_F\) an isomorphism, and how is the Gauss degree computed?
Assistant
The dimension count alone is not enough. A classical theorem gives \[ \Lambda^2H^1(F,\mathbf Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2(F,\mathbf Q), \] hence on the \((2,0)\)-part \[ \lambda_F:\Lambda^2H^0(F,\Omega_F^1)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^0(F,K_F). \] The tangent-bundle theorem identifies the Albanese Gauss map with the natural embedding \[ F\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Gr}(2,5), \] so \(\deg\gamma_F=1\). Classically, the Plücker/canonical degree \(45\) is obtained via incidence divisors. If \(D_K\) is the divisor of lines meeting a plane \(K\), then \(D_K\sim K_F\). For incidence divisors \(D_s,D_t\) attached to general lines one has \(D_sD_t=5\), and a special plane section gives \(D_K\equiv3D_s\). Thus \[ K_F^2=D_K^2=9D_s^2=45. \]

6. The Schoen surface

User
Why is \(\lambda_S\) surjective, and how do we get the degree of the Gauss image?
Assistant
For a Schoen surface, \[ q=4,\qquad p_g=5, \] so the wedge map is \(\mathbf C^6\to\mathbf C^5\), hence its kernel has dimension at least \(1\). For a general Schoen surface, \(\rho(S)=1\), so there is no fibration onto a curve: a fibre would have square \(0\), impossible in a rank-one Néron–Severi group generated by an ample class. If \(\dim\ker\lambda_S\ge2\), then its projectivization contains a line in \(\mathbf P^5\), which meets the Klein quadric \(\operatorname{Gr}(2,4)\). That gives a decomposable kernel element and, by Castelnuovo–de Franchis, an irrational pencil. Therefore \[ \dim\ker\lambda_S=1,\qquad \lambda_S\text{ is surjective}, \] and the generator is non-decomposable. The Gauss/canonical image lies in a smooth quadric \(Q^3\subset\mathbf P^4\). A degeneration of the canonical image shows that it is a \((2,4)\) complete intersection of degree \(8\). Since \(K_S^2=16\), \[ \deg\gamma_S=2. \]

7. General strategy via the Gauss cycle

User
Is there a common strategy to determine the degree of the Gauss image?
Assistant
Compute the pushed-forward Gauss cycle \[ (\gamma_Z)_*[Z]=d[Y]\in A_*(\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)). \] If \(\mathcal U\) is the tautological bundle, then for an immersion \[ \gamma_Z^*\mathcal U=T_Z. \] Hence Schubert intersections on \(Y\) are Chern numbers on \(Z\): \[ \int_Z s_\lambda(\Omega_Z^1)=d\int_Y\sigma_\lambda. \] The simplest case is \[ K_Z^r=d\,\deg_{\rm Pl}(Y). \] One then uses example-specific geometry—incidence geometry for Fano, degeneration for Schoen—to identify \([Y]\) or enough of it to separate \(d\) from \([Y]\).

8. Moving hyperplanes and conormal monodromy

User
For monodromy I mean that \(H\subset V\) moves and \[ \gamma_Z^{-1}(\operatorname{Gr}(r,H)) \] is generically finite; these points permute.
Assistant
For \([\alpha]\in\mathbf P(V^\vee)\), let \(H_\alpha=\ker\alpha\). Then \[ \gamma_Z(z)\in\operatorname{Gr}(r,H_\alpha) \iff (\iota_Z^*\alpha)(z)=0. \] Hence \[ \gamma_Z^{-1}(\operatorname{Gr}(r,H_\alpha)) = Z(\iota_Z^*\alpha). \] For an immersion the corresponding incidence space is \[ \mathbf P(N^*_{Z/A})\to\mathbf P(V^\vee), \] the conormal Gauss map, of degree \[ \int_Zc_r(\Omega_Z^1). \] Under Plücker, \[ \operatorname{Gr}(r,H_\alpha) = \operatorname{Gr}(r,V)\cap\mathbf P(\Lambda^rH_\alpha). \]

9. Geometric meaning of \(c_r\) and \(c_1^r\)

User
So \(c_r\) and \(c_1^r\) have special geometrical meaning?
Assistant
For an immersion, \[ (-1)^r\int_Zc_r(T_Z) = \deg(\text{conormal Gauss map}), \] while \[ (-1)^r\int_Zc_1(T_Z)^r = K_Z^r = \deg(\gamma_Z)\deg_{\rm Pl}(\gamma_Z(Z)). \] These correspond to the Schubert classes \(\sigma_{1^r}\) and \(\sigma_1^r\). The formulas hold for any immersion into an abelian variety, not only for the Albanese embedding.

10. Generically immersive maps: the real subtlety

User
If \(\iota_Z\) is only generically immersive, I am afraid closure adds extra points.
Assistant
The closure of the main conormal variety does not itself change the generic degree. The issue is that the full incidence \[ \mathcal I=\{(z,[\alpha]):(\iota_Z^*\alpha)(z)=0\} \] may acquire additional components supported over the rank-drop locus. Those can dominate \(\mathbf P(V^\vee)\) and contribute to a general fibre. For a rank-\(k\) stratum \(R\subset Z\) of dimension \(s\), the incidence over \(R\) has dimension \[ (n-1)+(s-k). \] Thus \(s-k\) is the excess dimension.

11. Exact tangent and conormal formulas with excess

User
I would like exact formulas for the tangent Gauss/Plücker formula and the conormal degree in the generically immersive case.
Assistant
Let \[ \varphi:V^\vee\otimes\mathcal O_Z\to\Omega_Z^1 \] and let \(B\) be the scheme defined by the maximal minors of \(\varphi\). Then \[ \operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^r\varphi)=\mathcal I_BK_Z. \] Let \[ \mu:\Gamma=\operatorname{Bl}_BZ\to Z, \qquad \mathcal I_B\mathcal O_\Gamma=\mathcal O_\Gamma(-E), \] and let \(\bar\gamma:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Gr}(r,V)\) be the resolved Gauss map. Then \[ \bar\gamma^*\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Gr}}(1) \simeq \mu^*K_Z\otimes\mathcal O_\Gamma(-E). \] Therefore \[ \boxed{ \deg(\gamma_Z)\deg_{\rm Pl}(Y) = \int_\Gamma(\mu^*K_Z-E)^r. } \] On the conormal side, if \(\mathcal U\) denotes the pullback of the tautological rank-\(r\) bundle to \(\Gamma\), then \[ \boxed{ \deg(\text{main conormal Gauss map}) = \int_\Gamma c_r(\mathcal U^\vee). } \] The difference from \(\int_Zc_r(\Omega_Z^1)\) is a localized top-Chern excess supported on the rank-drop locus.

12. Why the maximal-minor ideal appears

User
Why is \[ \operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^r\varphi)=\mathcal I_BK_Z? \] I do not know whether \(B\) has expected dimension.
Assistant
No expected-dimension hypothesis is needed. Locally trivialize \(\Omega_Z^1\) and write \(\varphi\) as an \(r\times n\) matrix \(A\). If \(\eta\) is a local generator of \(K_Z\), then \[ \Lambda^r\varphi(e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_r}) = \det(A_{i_1,\dots,i_r})\,\eta. \] So the image is exactly the ideal generated by all maximal minors, multiplied by \(\eta\): \[ \operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^r\varphi) = I_r(A)\eta = \mathcal I_BK_Z. \] Expected codimension is relevant for Porteous-type formulas, not for this identity.

13. Fitting ideals, in elementary language

User
I don't know what \(\operatorname{Fitt}_0\) is.
Assistant
If \[ R^m\xrightarrow{A}R^r\to M\to0, \] then \(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M)\) is the ideal generated by the \(r\times r\) minors of \(A\). It cuts out the support of \(M\), with scheme structure and multiplicity. In our situation, \[ \mathcal I_B = \operatorname{Fitt}_0(\operatorname{coker}\varphi). \] This is simply a coordinate-free name for the maximal-minor ideal.

14. What \(I_B\) remembers about \(\operatorname{Im} arphi\)

User
Can we describe \(\operatorname{Im}\varphi\) from \(I_B\)?
Assistant
Only its determinant defect. If \[ E=\operatorname{Im}\varphi\subset\Omega_Z^1, \] then \[ \operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^rE\to K_Z)=I_BK_Z. \] But \(I_B\) does not determine \(E\) itself. Over a DVR, \[ E=t^{a_1}Re_1\oplus\cdots\oplus t^{a_r}Re_r \] has \[ I_B=(t^{a_1+\cdots+a_r}), \] so the ideal remembers only the sum of the exponents, not the whole tuple.

15. Blow-up notation from the handwritten notes

User
Please use my notation setting for the blow-up example.
Assistant
In the handwritten notes, the blow-up of \(\mathbb C^n\) at \(0\) is written \[ \pi_2:\mathcal E_n\to\mathbb C^n, \] with exceptional divisor \[ L=\pi_2^{-1}(0)\simeq\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}. \] On the chart \(B_1\), \[ \pi_2(x_1,y_2,\dots,y_n) = (x_1,x_1y_2,\dots,x_1y_n), \] and \[ L\cap B_1=\{x_1=0\}. \] For \(I=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\), \[ I\mathcal O_{B_1} = (x_1,x_1y_2,\dots,x_1y_n) = (x_1). \] Thus the pulled-back ideal becomes invertible on the blow-up.

16. Pullback of an ideal under a ring map

User
How do I compute the pullback of an ideal \((f_1,f_2,\dots)\) when the map is \(A\to B\)?
Assistant
With the convention used in your notes, pull back the functions and generate an ideal in \(B\): \[ I=(f_1,\dots,f_m) \quad\leadsto\quad IB=(\phi(f_1),\dots,\phi(f_m)). \] This is the ideal of the scheme-theoretic inverse image. There is another object, \[ I\otimes_AB, \] the tensor-product pullback as a module. It can be larger because it may contain torsion. There is a natural map \[ I\otimes_AB\to B \] whose image is \(IB\).

17. Explicit local-chart comparison on the blow-up of \(\mathbf A^2\)

User
Could you explain explicitly, in local charts, the difference between the two pullbacks?
Assistant
Take \[ A=k[x,y],\qquad I=(x,y). \] On the \(x\)-chart of the blow-up, \[ B=k[x,t],\qquad y=xt. \] The pulled-back/extended ideal is \[ IB=(x,xt)=(x). \] For the tensor-product pullback, let \(e_x,e_y\) represent the generators \(x,y\). Downstairs the relation is \[ -y e_x+x e_y=0. \] After tensoring with \(B\), \[ x(e_y-te_x)=0. \] Thus \(e_y-te_x\) can survive as \(x\)-torsion supported on the exceptional divisor \(x=0\). The map \[ I\otimes_AB\to B \] kills this torsion and has image \((x)\). The \(y\)-chart is analogous.

18. Final notation convention

User
So in my local chart the pullback \(\mu^*I_Z\) is \[ (x_1,x_1y_2,x_1y_3)=(x_1). \] Why not just call that the pullback?
Assistant
We will. To match your notes and avoid unnecessary notation changes, from now on: \[ \boxed{ \mu^*I_Z := I_Z\mathcal O_{\widetilde Z} = (\text{pulled-back generators}). } \] Thus on the chart \[ \mu^*I_Z=(x_1,x_1y_2,x_1y_3)=(x_1), \] which is invertible. If the tensor-product object is ever needed, it will be written explicitly as \[ I_Z\otimes_{\mathcal O_Z}\mathcal O_{\widetilde Z}. \] This avoids ambiguity and follows the notation used in the discussion.
