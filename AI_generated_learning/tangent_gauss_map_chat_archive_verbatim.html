Assuming **AJ = Abel–Jacobi** and **AP = Abel–Prym**, there is a very clean conceptual thread that makes these five examples into a single talk rather than a catalogue. ### The organizing principle I would put one formula near the beginning and return to it throughout. Let \[ f:X^n\longrightarrow A \] be an immersion into an abelian variety, and put \[ V=H^0(A,\Omega_A^1). \] Translation identifies every \(T_{f(x)}A\) with \(T_0A=V^\vee\), so the differential gives the **tangent Gauss map** \[ \gamma_f:X\longrightarrow \operatorname{Gr}(n,V^\vee), \qquad x\longmapsto df_x(T_xX). \] After the Plücker embedding, the crucial point is that \[ X\xrightarrow{\gamma_f}\operatorname{Gr}(n,V^\vee) \hookrightarrow \mathbf P(\wedge^nV^\vee) \] is exactly the rational map defined by the image of \[ \boxed{\quad \wedge^n V\longrightarrow H^0(X,K_X). \quad} \] That can be the thesis of the talk: > **The tangent Gauss map is the projective geometry of the canonical forms obtained by wedging the invariant \(1\)-forms of the ambient abelian variety.** Then all five examples become variations on what this space of canonical forms looks like. | Example | Ambient forms | Tangent Gauss map | |---|---|---| | Abel–Jacobi \(C\to J(C)\) | \(H^0(K_C)\) | canonical map | | Abel–Prym | Prym differentials | Prym-canonical map | | Fano surface \(S\subset\operatorname{Alb}(S)\) | \(\wedge^2H^0(\Omega_S^1)\simeq H^0(K_S)\) | natural \(S\to \mathrm{Gr}(2,5)\) | | Schoen surface \(S\subset\operatorname{Alb}(S)\) | \(\wedge^2H^0(\Omega_S^1)\twoheadrightarrow H^0(K_S)\), kernel \(1\)-dimensional | canonical map inside a hyperplane section of \(\mathrm{Gr}(2,4)\) | | spin curve \(X\to A\) | \(W=\operatorname{Im}(\operatorname{Sym}^2H^0(L)\to H^0(K_X))\) | Veronese of the spin pencil | For the **AJ example**, this is almost the definition. For \[ C\hookrightarrow J(C), \qquad T_0J(C)=H^0(K_C)^\vee, \] the tangent line at \(p\) corresponds to evaluation of canonical forms at \(p\). Thus \[ \gamma_{AJ}:C\to \mathbf P(H^0(K_C)^\vee) \] is precisely the canonical map. This is the ideal first example because there is essentially no machinery: “differentiate Abel–Jacobi and get the canonical curve.” The **Abel–Prym example** should come immediately afterwards. For an étale double cover \[ \pi:\widetilde C\to C \] with associated \(2\)-torsion \(\eta\), the relevant invariant differentials on the Prym correspond to \[ H^0(C,K_C\otimes\eta). \] Differentiating the Abel–Prym map gives tangent directions that agree projectively at the two points over \(p\), so the tangent Gauss map factors through \(C\), and the resulting map \[ C\longrightarrow \mathbf P H^0(K_C\otimes\eta)^\vee \] is the Prym-canonical map. Then I would say: **now increase the dimension of \(X\)**. For the **Fano surface** \(S=F(Y)\) of lines on a smooth cubic threefold \(Y\subset\mathbf P^4\), one has \(q(S)=5\), so \[ \gamma_S:S\to \operatorname{Gr}(2,5). \] Here the beautiful point is that this abstract tangent Gauss map is the already familiar geometric map \[ [\ell]\longmapsto [\ell]\in\operatorname{Gr}(2,5) \] coming from the fact that \(S\) parametrizes the lines of \(Y\). This is the tangent-bundle theorem. Moreover the Plücker composite \[ S\to\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^9 \] is the canonical map of \(S\). That makes Fano a particularly strong example: the tangent Gauss map **recovers the original incidence geometry** from the Albanese embedding. The **Schoen surface** should probably be the climax of the surface part. Yes. I would make the **general theory of a map to an abelian variety** the subject of the first half, and then present the examples as increasingly structured instances of that theory. The key distinction is: - **full Albanese examples:** Abel–Jacobi, Fano, Schoen; - **quotients of the Albanese:** Abel–Prym and your spin-curve \(J(X)\twoheadrightarrow A\). That gives the talk a very natural architecture. ## 1. General setup: a map to an abelian variety Let \(X\) be a smooth projective \(n\)-fold and \[ f:X\longrightarrow A \] a morphism to an abelian variety, assumed to have rank \(n\) generically. Put \[ V_A=H^0(A,\Omega_A^1),\qquad W_f=\operatorname{Im}\bigl(f^*:V_A\to H^0(X,\Omega_X^1)\bigr). \] On the open set \(U\subset X\) where \(df\) has rank \(n\), evaluation gives \[ W_f\otimes\mathcal O_U\twoheadrightarrow \Omega_U^1. \] This is, in my view, the best **definition** of the tangent Gauss map: \[ \gamma_f:U\longrightarrow \operatorname{Gr}(n,W_f), \] where you use the quotient convention for the Grassmannian. Dually it sends \[ x\longmapsto df_x(T_xX)\subset W_f^\vee. \] Already here I would emphasize: > The tangent Gauss map depends only on the space of invariant differentials of the ambient abelian variety that survive on \(X\). So the central object is really the pair \[ (X,W),\qquad W\subset H^0(X,\Omega_X^1), \] when \(W\) comes from a map to an abelian variety. --- ## 2. The fundamental Plücker proposition This should be your first proposition. Taking determinants of \[ W_f\otimes\mathcal O_X\to\Omega_X^1 \] gives \[ \lambda_f: \bigwedge^n W_f\longrightarrow H^0(X,K_X), \qquad \omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n \longmapsto \omega_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_n. \] Then: \[ \boxed{ \operatorname{Pl}\circ\gamma_f \text{ is the rational map defined by } \operatorname{Im}\lambda_f\subset H^0(K_X). } \] This is the basic dictionary for the entire talk. From it you immediately get three pieces of information: \[ W_f \quad\rightsquigarrow\quad \bigwedge^nW_f \overset{\lambda_f}{\longrightarrow} H^0(K_X). \] The three things to study are therefore \[ \boxed{ W_f,\qquad \ker\lambda_f,\qquad \operatorname{Im}\lambda_f. } \] And geometrically: - \(W_f\) tells you which differentials are visible to \(A\); - \(\operatorname{Im}\lambda_f\) is the canonical subsystem defining the Plücker Gauss map; - \(\ker\lambda_f\) gives the **linear equations cutting the Gauss image inside the Plücker Grassmannian**. That last point will lead perfectly to Schoen. --- # 3. Albanese universality Now bring in the Albanese, rather than starting with it. Choose a base point. Translation is irrelevant, so everything depends on the homomorphism of abelian varieties and hence on the selected space \(W\). The surviving differentials are \[ W= H^0(K_{\widetilde C})^{-} \simeq H^0(K_C\otimes\eta). \] Therefore the tangent Gauss map is the map defined by the anti-invariant canonical differentials.
